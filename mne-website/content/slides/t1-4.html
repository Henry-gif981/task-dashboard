1.3. Business strategies of MNEs

1.3.1. Generic international strategies of MNEs (cont.)

Table 1.4. Generic international strategies of MNEs
StrategyLogicAdvantagesDisadvantagesTypical examples
InternationalTransfer home-country products and know-how to foreign markets with limited adaptationLow cost of adaptation; leverages core competenceWeak local fit; little cost optimizationMicrosoft or Pfizer in early foreign expansion
Multi-domestic (localization)Customize products and marketing for each national market; subsidiaries are largely autonomousHigh local responsivenessHigh cost; duplication; little learning across unitsUnilever, Nestlé (historically), many food and consumer-goods firms
Global (standardization)Offer standardized products and concentrate production to exploit scale and experience curveLowest cost; consistent brandPoor adaptation to local needsIntel, Samsung semiconductors, Apple devices
TransnationalAchieve efficiency, local responsiveness and worldwide learning simultaneously through a networkCombines all advantagesOrganizationally complex and difficult to implementToyota, Caterpillar, Procter & Gamble (increasingly)

Source: adapted from Bartlett and Ghoshal (1989) and Hill and Hult (2019).
